Six Measured CERs for 2026–27: Current Picks and Alternatives

Our Biology team plans six claim–evidence–reasoning tasks this year, built and scored the same way so growth shows from the first to the last. Each one gives every student a short reading and a table of real data. The six topics in the September 27 draft are good ideas to consider. Beside each one sit two researched alternatives, the data each would use, and a recommendation. If the team likes the current picks, they stand. If it wants different ones, we build different CERs, from these alternatives or from new ideas.

One timing change is proposed. No common CER before Monday, October 26, so the first scored task meets students after they have practiced the format. Unit 2 ends October 20, which moves its slot out. The proposal fills the sixth slot with Unit 6 Genetics, which keeps all six inside the first three quarters, ahead of spring SOL review. The IV-saline topic from the draft stays available as optional Unit 2 practice.

Every number below was read on the source linked in its row. A note marked check is a figure in the draft that could not be confirmed and should change before the task is printed.

Slot 1 · Unit 3 Cell Energetics · Oct 26–29

SOL BIO.2c, BIO.2e

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Current pickWhy dough rises when warm and stops in the ovenBaker’s yeast grows from about 2.8 °C to 45.4 °C, fastest near 32 °C (Salvadó et al. 2011). In dough, gas output keeps rising to about 40.5 °C, and the yeast is dead by 60 °C (BAKERpedia).Growth peaks near 32 °C but gas output peaks near 40 °C. Keep the two apart in the items. The 60 °C figure comes from a baking reference, not a journal.
Alternative AHow a hummingbird’s breath shows the sugar it just drank being burnedThe ratio of carbon dioxide out to oxygen in climbs from 0.74 after a fast to 1.01 after 40 minutes of feeding; 81–83% of the fuel is the sugar just eaten (Welch & Suarez 2007).Respiration only, no enzyme temperature. The birds measured are western species, not Virginia’s ruby-throated.
Alternative BWhy Virginia sets a minimum light level for the Bay’s underwater grassesThe state standard asks for 22% of surface light at the grass in salty water and 13% in fresh (9VAC25-260-185). From the standard’s own formula, light 1 m down is 5.5% in murky water and 48.4% in clear.Photosynthesis against all-day respiration. The light percentages are calculated from the regulation; label them that way.

Recommendation: keep the yeast dough. It is the only option that also measures enzymes and temperature, which no Unit 1 test covered.

Slot 2 · Unit 4 Nucleic Acids and Protein Synthesis · Dec 1–4

SOL BIO.2d, BIO.5a

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Current pickHow one DNA base change causes sickle cell diseaseCodon GAG becomes GTG, and glutamic acid becomes valine (MedlinePlus). About 100,000 people in the U.S. have the disease; 1 in 13 Black or African American babies is born with sickle cell trait (CDC).Older sources and the SOL call it position 6; current gene naming counts one more and says position 7. A one-line footnote prevents confusion.
Alternative AWhy a lizard’s venom protein lasts longer in the body than the human hormone it copiesHuman GLP-1 lasts 1–2 minutes (Müller et al. 2019). Exenatide, from Gila monster venom, lasts 2.4 hours; semaglutide about a week (FDA Byetta and Ozempic labels).Tests sequence and function more than codon reading. Weight-loss drugs are a sensitive topic with teenagers, so keep the focus on the protein.
Alternative BWhy death-cap poisoning shows up hours after the mealFourteen California patients in 2016; symptoms began 9–10 hours after eating; three needed liver transplants (CDC MMWR 2017). The toxin stops RNA polymerase II, the enzyme that makes mRNA.A strong transcription case. Serious illness is involved; give no identification tips.

Recommendation: keep sickle cell. It is the option that asks students to read a codon chart, the skill this slot was chosen to measure. The Gila monster story can run as a practice reading instead.

Slot 3 · Unit 5 Cell Growth, Division and Specialization · Jan 11–15

SOL BIO.3c, BIO.3e (Alternative B adds BIO.5d)

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Current pickWhy skin heals in weeks and heart muscle does notGut lining replaces itself in about 3.5 days (Darwich et al. 2014); red blood cells last up to 120 days (NCBI); new skin cells reach the surface in 4–5 weeks (Lindwall et al. 2006); about 1% of heart muscle cells are replaced each year at age 25 (Bergmann et al. 2009).The heart figure is debated among researchers. Print it as an estimate.
Alternative AHow a single stem cell rebuilt a whole flatwormAll 78 irradiated worms given no cells died. Of 130 given one stem cell, 7 were rescued, and the rescued worms carried the donor’s DNA (Wagner et al. 2011).Specialization comes from which genes are used, not which are kept. “Stem cell” needs a sentence of definition.
Alternative BWhy Virginia allows only grass carp with three sets of chromosomes in pondsNormal grass carp carry 48 chromosomes; the permitted fish carry 72 and are meant to be sterile (Virginia DWR). No fish with two sets turned up among 1,200 certified fish checked (USGS).Brings meiosis into the slot, a gap the draft names. Virginia case.

Recommendation: keep skin and heart. Choose the grass carp instead if the team wants meiosis measured in this slot.

Slot 4 · Unit 6 Genetics and Heredity · week of Feb 8 (new slot)

SOL BIO.5c · replaces the Unit 2 slot

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Proposed pickWhy type O is the most common blood type when its allele is recessiveU.S. frequencies: O+ 37.4%, A+ 35.7%, B+ 8.5%, AB+ 3.4%, O− 6.6%, A− 6.3%, B− 1.5%, AB− 0.6% (Stanford Blood Center). The O allele makes an inactive enzyme (Yamamoto et al. 1990).Three alleles, codominance and the “dominant means common” mistake in one table. Treat Rh as a second gene.
Alternative AHow two genes together set a Labrador’s coat colorOf 33,320 UK Labradors, 44.6% were black, 27.8% yellow and 23.8% chocolate; chocolate dogs lived a median 10.7 years against 12.1 for the others (O’Neill et al. 2018).One gene hiding another goes beyond the SOL bullet. Why chocolate dogs die younger is not settled.
Alternative BHow a male cat can be tortoiseshellOf 4,598 male cats, 20 were tortoiseshell. Of 11 tested: 6 had cells of two kinds, 2 were XXY, 2 XX, 1 XY (Leaman et al. 1999).Reasoning from chromosomes rather than a memorized rule. XXY ties back to Unit 5.

Recommendation: ABO blood types. Its table scores cleanly from a key and matches the bullet directly.

The IV-saline topic, if kept as Unit 2 practice: no published table of red-cell bursting at each salt level was found. Use the verified points: bursting begins at 0.45% salt, half the cells have burst at 0.425%, all by 0.30% (Parpart et al. 1947). Saline’s 308 and blood’s 275–295 are measured in different units and should not sit side by side.

Slot 5 · Unit 7 Evolution · Mar 8–12

SOL BIO.7b, BIO.7c

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Current pickWhy finch beaks deepened after the 1977 droughtAbout 1,200 medium ground finches lived on Daphne Major before the drought, and more than 80% died (HHMI BioInteractive). Birds with deep beaks survived best (Boag & Grant 1981).Check: the draft’s 9.42 to 9.96 mm was not found in any source. Published survivor means range from 9.82 to 9.84 mm. HHMI’s free dataset of 100 measured birds gives figures that can be cited.
Alternative AWhy white-nose syndrome nearly erased one Virginia bat species and spared othersIn 18 western Virginia caves, 2008–2013: little brown bats fell 99.0%, tri-colored 89.5%, Indiana 33.5% (USGS). Big brown bats were not significantly affected (Virginia DWR).Virginia case. Evidence of selection in the survivors is recent; present it as a hypothesis being tested.
Alternative BHow the dark peppered moth rose and then fellThe mutation behind the dark form dates to about 1819 (van’t Hof et al. 2016). In 4,864 moths released over six years, birds took dark moths more often on today’s cleaner bark (Cook et al. 2012).Selection reversing when the environment changes. Historic percentages were not verified.

Recommendation: keep the finches, with HHMI’s numbers in the table. Choose the bats if the team wants a Virginia case.

Slot 6 · Unit 8 Classification and Biodiversity · Mar 30–Apr 2

SOL BIO.6a, BIO.6f, BIO.7a

OptionStudents explainData for the tableNotes
Current pickWhat differences in one protein show about relationshipsCytochrome c differences from the human protein: chimpanzee 0, rhesus monkey 1, horse 12, chicken 13, tuna 21 (checked against UniProt; Britannica).Check: textbooks give yeast 44; the current sequence gives about 39. Print “about 40” or leave yeast out.
Alternative AWhy one kind of microbe was moved out of the bacteriaIn ribosomal RNA, a methane-making microbe matched E. coli at 0.11 and yeast at 0.11, but another methane-maker at 0.51 (Woese & Fox 1977).Classification changing with new evidence, and the three domains. The similarity score needs one sentence of explanation.
Alternative BWhy giraffes were split into four speciesDNA from 190 giraffes showed four groups that separated 1.25 to 1.99 million years ago (Fennessy et al. 2016). The IUCN accepted four species in August 2025 (GCF).Species defined by genetic isolation rather than looks. Population counts need confirming before use.

Recommendation: keep cytochrome c, with the yeast row corrected.

May, unmeasured · Unit 10 Ecology

Two Virginia cases with checked data, either of which would work: the hemlock woolly adelgid, which killed about 95% of Shenandoah National Park’s hemlocks by 2003 (NPS); and James River bald eagles, which went from no breeding pairs in 1977 to more than 340 by 2021 (William & Mary).
