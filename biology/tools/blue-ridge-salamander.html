Blue Ridge Salamander Project

Turn over a rock in the Blue Ridge, find the animal's DNA, and decode it gene by gene. The animal stays hidden until every gene checks correct.

▶ Watch the project start to finish on YouTube (9 min): turning the rock for a Creature Code, finding and decoding the genes, checking the ember gene for a new mutation, and revealing the salamander.

Quarter 2: decode your salamander

  1. Start hereTurn the Rock and DecodeRoll for a site, turn a rock, roll for the lab slip, get a Creature Code. Check each gene you decode on paper.
  2. With your codeFind the GenesFifteen pairs of chromosomes. Highlight the TATA box, the start codon, the stop codon and the part that codes for the protein on each strip.
  3. Whole classCodon BingoOne caller, a different card on every iPad, and a round played on your own animal.
  4. When you finishYour Salamander in 3DEnter your keeper code to see your animal’s reveal, on this device or any other.
  5. PreviewThe Animal in 3DFour sample animals at night, with sound. A device check.

Quarter 3: Salamander Families Coming in January

Two salamanders, one female and one male, start a family. A roll of the die decides which chromosome of each pair goes into the egg and which goes into the sperm. Four eggs hatch, and the genes explain who looks like whom.

  1. Open nowGenes in MotionFour sample animals. Every gene changes how an animal moves. Make one walk, tap it, hear its call, and send a red insect past its snout.
  2. After your revealYour Own Salamander’s Genes at WorkOpen your animal with your keeper code and find each of its genes in the way it moves.
  3. JanuarySalamander FamiliesPair up, roll for each chromosome pair, and watch the egg and the sperm form and meet.
  4. JanuaryThe Karyotype CardA hatchling’s fifteen pairs of chromosomes line up. Count the pairs and find the sex pair.
  5. JanuaryHatchlings in 3DEach baby opens from its own egg code, small and already awake.
  6. JanuaryThe Punnett Lab and the Family PosterPredict with Punnett squares, compare with your four real eggs, and build the family’s poster with a pedigree.
