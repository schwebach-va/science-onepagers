Energy Check

Chapter 6 comes down to two questions a cell answers before anything else happens: which reactions can run at all, and how the ones it needs are made to run fast enough. Twenty-four questions sort out which of the eight ideas you already own. About twenty minutes.

Nothing is submitted. No score leaves this iPad and I never see it. Answer honestly and the result is worth something.

Three questions on each idea. ATP and coupling gets a tile of its own, because that is where the free-energy bookkeeping turns into something a cell actually does, and it is the step people most often skip.

How the Checkpoint asks Chapter 6

Checkpoint Chapter 6 sits on the NOVA Canvas Quizzes tab. Unlimited attempts, highest score kept, and it shows the right answer after every try. Take it cold, work the drawings above, take it again. Seven of its thirty-seven questions put a graph in front of you — an energy diagram with its arrows lettered, a rate curve that levels off, temperature and pH profiles — and ask you to read ΔG, activation energy or an optimum straight off it, so practise on your own drawings until the arrows mean something without a label.

One thing in Unit 6 is built to be handled rather than read. The Water Patch, Module 2, folds a protein in water and then runs an enzyme through induced fit, hydrolysis and release, one step at a time. When Unit 7 starts, the Energy Patch follows the ATP this chapter spends back to where a cell makes it.
