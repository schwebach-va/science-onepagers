Daughter or son: XX is a female, XY is a male. The mother always gives an X; the sperm decides with an X or a Y.

Genes that share a chromosome usually travel together: ember color and skin toxin (chromosome 2), glow and claws (chromosome 4), call and color vision (chromosome X). Crossing over can split them: while partner chromosomes are paired in meiosis, one strand from each partner can trade ends, so one egg or sperm gets a mixed strand. A male has one X, so his call and color-vision genes never split.

Dual enrollment, map distance: one map unit is 1% of eggs or sperm with a mixed strand. Ember color to toxin is 10 map units, call to color vision 20, glow to claws 30. The farther apart two genes sit, the more often they split.

Model choices: these genes, alleles and powers belong to the salamanders of this project. Real red-backed salamanders do have a red-backed form and a lead-backed form, and albino animals turn up in the wild.
